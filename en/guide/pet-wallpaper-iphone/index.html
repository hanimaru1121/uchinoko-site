Pet Wallpaper for iPhone: Live Photos, Lock Screen Tricks, and Photos That Look Great

Published October 8, 2026

Your wallpaper is the thing you see more than anything else on your phone, so why not make it your pet? This guide covers the iPhone features that make pet wallpapers shine, and how to pick photos that look great.

The basics

  1. Open Settings → Wallpaper → Add New Wallpaper.
  2. Tap Photos and choose a photo of your pet.
  3. Pinch and drag to position it, then tap Add.
  4. Choose whether to use it for both the Lock Screen and Home Screen, or set a different Home Screen.

You can also touch and hold the Lock Screen and tap the "+" button. Button names may vary slightly between iOS versions.

Four features that make pet wallpapers better

1. A moving wallpaper with a Live Photo

Choose a Live Photo and turn on the play button, and your pet moves briefly on the Lock Screen each time your iPhone wakes. Short moments work best: a tail wag, a head tilt. Not every Live Photo is eligible, so some won't animate.

2. Photo Shuffle: change your wallpaper automatically

Choose Photo Shuffle and the Lock Screen rotates through photos. You can have it change on tap, on lock, hourly, or daily. Point it at an album of just your pet, or pick the photos by hand, and you'll see only them.

3. Your pet in front of the clock

With some photos, the depth effect lets your pet's head overlap the clock, as if stepping in front of it. It works best when your pet stands out clearly from the background, and won't work with every photo.

4. Spatial Scenes for a 3D look

On iPhone 12 or later with iOS 26 or later, you can turn a photo into a Spatial Scene that shifts with depth as you tilt your phone. The 3D effect appears on the Lock Screen only. Again, photos where your pet is clearly separated from the background work best.

Picking photos that look great

  • Portrait orientation. Landscape photos get cropped heavily on both sides.
  • Room at the top. The clock sits at the top of the Lock Screen, so leave some space above your pet's face.
  • A clean background. A plain wall, floor or lawn makes your pet pop and helps the depth effect kick in.
  • Bright and sharp. Blur is much more noticeable on a full screen.

Want to use a video?

iPhone can't use a regular video as wallpaper on its own. Apps that convert video into a Live Photo (intoLive, for example) let you use method 1 above.

What about your Mac?

macOS includes animated wallpapers made by Apple, while photos you choose from your library work as still wallpapers through the built-in wallpaper settings. If you want your pet moving on your Mac, look at a desktop pet instead.

Combine wallpaper and widgets

Wallpaper goes big; widgets stay small. Set your pet as your Home Screen wallpaper and add a pet widget on top for a screen that makes you smile every time. Uchinoko Live brings your pet to your iPhone Home Screen and Mac desktop (learn more; iPhone app: US App Store only).
