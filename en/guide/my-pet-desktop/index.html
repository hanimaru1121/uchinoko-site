How to Turn Your Own Pet Into a Desktop Pet: Make It Yourself or Have It Made

Published October 8, 2026

What if the cat walking across your screen weren't just any cat, but yours? There are two ways to get there: make it yourself, or have it made. This guide compares them and lists what to look for so the result actually looks like your pet.

Two ways to do it

1. Make it yourself with an app that imports photos or video

Some Mac desktop pet apps let you import your own photos, videos or GIFs and put them on screen (see the comparison table). Costs vary by app, and you may be able to try one right away.

The trade-off: preparing the footage and making it look good is your job. If it doesn't quite look like your pet or doesn't move the way you hoped, it's yours to fix, and the result depends on how much time you're willing to put in.

2. Have it made, just for your pet

You send photos and someone else builds it. It takes longer and costs more, but you don't have to do the hands-on work. Some services let you review the likeness and request changes; check the revision policy before ordering. Uchinoko Live works this way (learn more).

Five things that make it look like your pet

Whichever route you take, check these when you see the result. They're usually what makes someone in the family say "Oh, that's Milo."

  • The face. Eye size and spacing, muzzle length. Tiny differences turn your pet into someone else's.
  • Markings and colors. Where the patches are, white socks, the tip of the tail. Make sure left and right aren't swapped.
  • Ear shape and position. Upright or floppy, or one ear folded.
  • Build. Round or lean. Coat length changes the impression a lot.
  • Signature poses. How they sit, how they sprawl, where the tail goes. This is where personality shows.

Before you hand over your photos

Whether it's an app or a service, if your pet's photos are going somewhere, look for answers to these:

  • Where the photos are sent: do they stay on your Mac, or get uploaded to a server?
  • When they're deleted, and whether they'll be used for anything else (samples, training)
  • Whether AI is used, and whether that's stated clearly
  • For made-to-order services: what happens if it doesn't look like your pet (revisions, refunds)

Which should you choose?

If you just want to see what it's like, try an app that imports photos first. If you then find yourself wanting a closer likeness, or don't have time to fiddle with it, look at having it made. Trying an app first can help you decide whether a custom service is worth considering.
