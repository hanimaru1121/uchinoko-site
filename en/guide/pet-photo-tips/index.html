How to Take Better Pet Photos With Your Phone: Light, Eye Level, Focus and Fast Movers

Published October 8, 2026

You try to catch your pet being adorable and get a blur, a dark smudge, or the back of their head. A few simple habits make a huge difference, and you don't need a fancy camera. Here's how to take better pet photos with the phone you already have.

Light: soft window light wins

Light matters more than anything else. Daylight near a window beats overhead room lights: the coat looks better and you get less blur. If the sun is harsh, a sheer curtain softens it nicely. Outdoors, look for open shade under a tree or next to a building.

Avoid the flash when you can. It tends to make eyes glow, and some pets are startled by bright light, so favor natural light.

Eye level: get down to their height

Shooting down from standing height gives you the classic big-head, short-legs photo. Crouch or lie on the floor and shoot at your pet's eye level, and their expression comes alive. For small pets, wait until they're up on the couch or bed.

Focus: on the eyes

The eyes make the photo. Tap your pet's eyes on screen to focus there. On iPhone, touch and hold to lock focus and exposure (AE/AF Lock), which helps when your pet is settled and you want to take your time.

For pets that won't sit still: burst and Live Photos

  • Burst mode. On iPhone, slide the shutter button to the left to shoot a burst (on supported models). You can also set the volume-up button to take bursts in Settings → Camera. Pick the best frame afterward.
  • Live Photos. They capture a moment before and after you press the shutter, so if you were a split second late, you can choose a better frame as the Key Photo when editing in the Photos app.
  • More light. In dim light your phone uses slower shutter speeds, which means more blur. The faster your pet, the more light you need.

Getting them to look at the camera

  • Wiggle a toy or make a small sound right above the lens to pull their gaze toward it.
  • Save their name for the key moment. Call it too often and they'll tune you out.
  • If you use treats, follow through after the shot. Photo time becomes a good time.
  • Stop if they're not enjoying it. Several short sessions beat one long one.

Watch out for wide-angle and zoom

Getting close with the ultra wide (0.5x) lens makes the nose look huge and changes the shape of the face. Fun for a silly shot, but for a true-to-life portrait, step back a little and use 1x or 2x. On many iPhone models, Portrait mode also works with dogs and cats and softly blurs the background.

Black and white coats

  • Black coats. Dark fur can lose detail, especially against a bright background. Tap near your pet's eyes to focus, then slide the sun icon up to brighten a little if needed.
  • White coats. The opposite: fur can blow out to pure white and lose its texture. Slide the sun icon down a bit to keep the detail.

Wipe the lens first

Your phone's lens picks up smudges in your pocket or bag, and smudges are one common reason photos look hazy. Give it a quick wipe with a soft cloth before you shoot.

Put your favorites on your screen

When you get a great shot, keep it in an album of just your pet. It's handy for your wallpaper and widgets. If you're choosing photos to turn into art or video, see How to animate a photo of your pet.
